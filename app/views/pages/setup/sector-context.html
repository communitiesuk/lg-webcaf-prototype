Our council is committed to enhancing the wellbeing and prosperity of our diverse community by providing responsive and accountable government services, fostering economic growth, and promoting a safe and thriving environment for residents.

Our council's objectives are:

  • economic development – to stimulate economic growth and job creation through strategic initiatives, business incentives, and infrastructure investments that attract and retain businesses while fostering entrepreneurship and innovation
  • community wellbeing – to promote the health, safety, and quality of life for residents by investing in essential services such as healthcare, public safety, parks, and recreational facilities. Collaborate with community partners to address social issues and support vulnerable populations
  • infrastructure enhancement – to enhance the council's infrastructure to support sustainable growth and development. Prioritise investments in transportation, utilities, and public facilities to improve accessibility, connectivity, and liveability for residents and businesses
  • environmental stewardship – to protect and preserve the council's natural resources, ecosystems, and biodiversity. Implement policies and programmes to mitigate the impacts of climate change, promote renewable energy, and ensure sustainable land use practices

These objectives help us fulfil our mission and serve the public interest by guiding the allocation of resources, formulation of policies, and implementation of programmes to deliver services efficiently, equitably, and transparently.

Our mission and objectives align with the delivery of government services by prioritising the wellbeing, prosperity, and safety of residents.

By focusing on these objectives – as well as fiscal responsibility and community engagement - the council aims to provide government services that meet the evolving needs of the community and remain available, reliable and secure.

Threat actors that could attack our organisation

Threat actors that could attack our organisation include:

  • cyber-criminal organisations - organised groups with the intent to steal sensitive information, disrupt services, or extort ransom payments through cyber-attacks such as ransomware, phishing, and malware campaigns
  • nation-state actors - governments or state-sponsored entities targeting government organisations for espionage, sabotage, or political motivations
  • insider threats - malicious or negligent insiders, including employees, contractors, or trusted partners, who may abuse their access privileges or inadvertently compromise sensitive information
  • hacktivist groups - ideologically motivated individuals or groups seeking to protest or disrupt government operations through cyberattacks, website defacement, or distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks

Why we are a target

We are a target due to:

  • public sector relevance - as a local government organisation, we handle a vast amount of sensitive information, including personal data of residents, financial records, and critical infrastructure details. This makes the council an attractive target for cybercriminals seeking to steal valuable data or disrupt operations
  • reliance on external suppliers, shared systems and digital services to deliver council services - the compromise or disruption of a third party could interrupt service delivery, provide a route into the council or affect multiple councils that rely on the same provider
  • the impact of essential service disruption - disrupting council services, such as emergency response systems, utilities, or public transportation, could have significant repercussions on public safety, economic stability, and community wellbeing. This makes the council an appealing target for threat actors aiming to cause disruption or chaos
  • political or ideological motivations - hacktivist groups or nation-state actors may target the council to advance political agendas, protest government policies, or undermine public confidence in government institutions

Potential consequences of successful attacks

Potential consequences of successful attacks include:

  • data breaches - unauthorised access to sensitive information, including personally identifiable information (PII), financial records, and confidential government documents, could lead to identity theft, fraud, and reputational damage for the council
  • service disruption - significant disruption or loss of council essential services, including but not limited to social care, revenue and benefits, housing or electoral services
  • harm to residents - disruption to essential services, loss of access to information or the use of inaccurate information could cause financial, physical or welfare harm to residents, particularly vulnerable people who depend on time-critical council services
  • loss of data availability or integrity - data could be encrypted, deleted, altered or made unavailable, preventing staff from accessing reliable information and affecting the council's ability to deliver services and make informed decisions
  • financial and organisational impact - a successful attack could result in fraud, lost income, incident response and recovery costs, legal or regulatory consequences, diversion of staff from normal duties and loss of public confidence
